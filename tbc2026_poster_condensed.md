## HOOK

In 2003, it took 13 years and $2.7 billion to read one human genome. Today, AI trained on 9.3 trillion DNA letters is learning to read genomes the way ChatGPT reads English — and it's starting to write new ones.

## I. TOPIC BACKGROUND

Genomic foundation models are large AI systems trained on billions of DNA sequences from thousands of species. Just as ChatGPT learned English by reading the internet, these models learn the "grammar" of genomes — which patterns regulate genes, how mutations affect function, and what makes human DNA different from a bacterium's.

The core idea: given a stretch of DNA (A, T, G, C), predict what nucleotide comes next. By training on trillions of bases, the AI discovers the rules of molecular biology on its own.

These models can:
- Predict whether a mutation causes disease
- Design synthetic genes with specific functions
- Annotate newly sequenced genomes
- Transfer knowledge across species

Every prediction still needs lab validation — but discovery that once took years now takes hours.

## II. TECHNOLOGY TIMELINE

**1990** — Human Genome Project launches — a 13-year, $2.7B global effort to sequence human DNA

**2003** — Human genome completed — reading DNA is possible, but understanding it remains the challenge

**2017** — Transformer architecture invented (Vaswani et al.) — the engine behind all modern AI

**2021** — DNABERT — first language model applied to DNA (Zhou et al., Mila)

**2024** — Evo published in *Science* — 7B params, designs CRISPR systems from scratch (Hsu, Hie, Arc Institute)

**2025** — Evo 2 — 40B params, all domains of life, 1M base pair context (Arc Institute)

## III. BIOTECH INNOVATORS & ECONOMIC IMPACT

### Evo & Evo 2 — Arc Institute
Led by Patrick Hsu and Brian Hie. Arc Institute co-founded with a $650M endowment by Stripe CEO Patrick Collison. Evo 2 predicts variant effects across the human genome. Fully open-source — free for researchers worldwide.

**$650M endowment | Open source**

### Nucleotide Transformer — BioNTech / InstaDeep
DNA language model deployed in mRNA vaccine and cancer therapeutic design. BioNTech acquired InstaDeep for ~$680M in 2023 — one of the largest AI-biotech acquisitions ever.

**$680M acquisition | Deployed in mRNA vaccine design**

### BioNeMo — NVIDIA
GPU platform powering genomic AI across pharma. Partners include Genentech/Roche and Amgen. AI-in-genomics market: ~$2B today, projected $5–10B by 2030.

**$5–10B projected market by 2030**

### Illumina — The Data Engine
Sequencing machines that generate virtually all the DNA data these models train on. Dropped genome cost from $2.7B to under $200. Now investing in AI variant interpretation (SpliceAI, PrimateAI).

**$4.5B revenue | ~80% global market share**

## IV. ETHICAL, LEGAL & SOCIAL ISSUES

### Biosecurity
AI that generates realistic DNA could theoretically design pathogens. The 2024 U.S. Executive Order on AI flagged generative biology as a national security risk. Arc Institute uses red-teaming and responsible-release policies.

### Genomic Bias
Training data skews heavily toward European-ancestry genomes. Models may make worse predictions for underrepresented populations — risking amplification of health disparities in clinical genomics.

### Privacy & Consent
Biobank samples were collected before anyone imagined AI training. Genomic data can re-identify individuals. GINA protects against employer discrimination but doesn't cover life insurance.

### IP & Ownership
If AI designs a novel gene, who holds the patent? U.S. patent law requires a human inventor. Regulatory frameworks remain untested for generative biology.

### Access & Equity
Training a 40B-parameter model requires thousands of GPU-hours — resources only wealthy institutions can afford. Open-sourcing helps, but infrastructure and expertise gaps risk creating a two-tier system in genomic medicine.
